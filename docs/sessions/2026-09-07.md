## What happened

**Tier 6 closed.** The last unverified item was the voice gate hold. It turned
out the gate was never being reached: the tool guidance said `forget` "needs
their yes every time", and the model read that as an instruction to go and ask
out loud rather than to call the tool. Nothing unsafe happened, which is the
trap, because the confirmation is the only thing that writes to the log and the
inbox. A question asked in prose is a confirmation that leaves no trace.
Fixed with a generated "Tools that ask first" section built from the `confirm`
flag (`bd9396f`).

**7a, the orb** (`fa5ef50`). The prototype was promoted rather than rewritten:
its only input is a number, and no transport decision can change what a scalar
means. three.js is vendored because a CDN link is a black rectangle behind a
corporate proxy and a third-party request from a page showing account names.
Rendering it found two defects reading it would not have: the nebula plane was
deciding the background colour, so the corners were pure black instead of
`#0E0F13`, and wide shallow gradients were banding into visible rings.

**7b, the transport** (`6dd4f8b`). One port serves the page and the socket.
Building it found that `_build_agent` defaulted the gate to `TerminalGate`, so
any new caller would have inherited a gate that blocks on `input()` nobody can
see. Assembly moved to `assembly.py` where not passing a gate means
`DenyingGate`. The `Sec-WebSocket-Accept` constant was wrong by one character
and every unit test passed anyway, because they all agreed with each other and
with nothing outside; Chromium refusing the handshake was the only signal.

**7c, the glass shell** (`718e43b`). The browser now gets `SocketGate`, which
asks by opening a card. Two defects only a real browser found: `display: grid`
beats the browser's own `[hidden]` rule, so an answered card left an invisible
overlay that ate every click; and the entry animation and the endless float
cannot share an element.

**The morning brief, redesigned.** With `quiet_after_days` at 21 it flagged 41
of 58 accounts, longest first, topped by one at 215 days: most of the book,
sorted by how thoroughly each account had been abandoned. A threshold was being
asked to do a ranking's job. Now five lines in three buckets, ranked by tier,
then activity count, then recency of the lapse.
